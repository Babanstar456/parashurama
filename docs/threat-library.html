Why, rule by rule

    Threat library

    Fourteen contact types, from a recon quad to a large military UAV. The skill being trained is telling them apart from radar, the friendly check, the radio signal, behaviour and, when close enough, the camera. Pick one to see its clues.

    Effectors the trainee can use

    Each effector works only on drones it can actually affect, and only inside its reach. Choosing one that cannot work scores nothing. Bars show reach against the 3 km radar range.

    How the 100 points are earned

    Detect, 30 points

    Full 30 if tracking starts within 3 s of the first radar paint, falling evenly to 0 at 20 s.

    Identify, 30 points

    The trainee names the type and how sure they are: Likely, Confident or Certain. A correct call earns 25 to 29. A wrong call earns 1 to 4, plus 5 if friend or foe was still right. Being certain and wrong earns the least.

    Defeat, 40 points

    Doctrine rules

    Every point in the debrief cites one of these rules, so a trainee always knows why they gained or lost it.

    Conditions that make it harder

    Radar reaches 3 km. The camera confirms a drone up to 2.6 km by day and 2.0 km at night. Weather cuts both.

    Instructors can also add a radar fault (a 60° blind sector at 30% detection), a camera fault or a dead RF sensor, and set the drill at night or in urban terrain.

    Real-world missions

    Scripted drills based on public reporting. Each one teaches a single lesson.

    Air base at night

    Army / IAF air defence. Night, rural.

    Inspired by the Jammu Air Force Station attack (27 June 2021). Identify slow 2.4 GHz quadcopters from RF and behaviour and jam them before they hover over the asset.

    Border post in dense fog

    BSF. Day, fog.

    Fog cuts camera range below a kilometre. Trust the RF signal and slow approach, and don't jam your own patrol UAV.

    Airport in heavy rain

    CISF. Day, urban, rain.

    Many contacts are birds: hold on them. In a city the gun causes collateral damage; jam instead.

    Forward post: swarm in a dust storm

    Army air defence. Day, dust, swarm.

    The swarm and the loitering munition have no radio link, so jamming fails. One GNSS spoof hits the whole swarm; keep interceptors for what spoofing cannot stop.

    When a trainee counts as ready

    A learning model tracks mastery of each skill and condition (detect, identify, defeat, timing, night, swarm, faulty sensors, bad weather) after every drill. Commanders see it on the readiness board.
